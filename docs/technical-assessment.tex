\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

The proposed platform is a \textbf{modular monolith} with explicit Policy, Risk,
Notification, and CORE integration boundaries. This is the most responsible starting
point for the stated domain: policy and risk transitions need strong consistency, while
24/7 operation requires the legacy CORE to be isolated from the request path. The
design can scale horizontally and each module has a clean extraction seam if evidence
later justifies an independent service.

The executable solution implements every mandatory route and rule. All state-changing
operations update the relational model and create a CORE delivery intent in one database
transaction. A transactional outbox dispatcher then calls the required agnostic
WebLogic/CORE boundary with retries and a stable event identifier. A CORE outage
therefore does not lose an accepted business change or make the public API unavailable.

\section{Module 1 - System Design}

\subsection{Context, assumptions, and design goals}

The business owns two rental-policy products:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{INDIVIDUAL}: the tenant is policyholder and insured party; the landlord
    is beneficiary; exactly one rental risk exists.
  \item \textbf{COLECTIVA}: a real-estate agency or co-property administration is the
    policyholder; tenants are insured; landlords are beneficiaries; one or many rental
    risks exist.
\end{itemize}

For every policy, premium is calculated as monthly rent multiplied by the initial
duration in months. Renewal applies IPC as a percentage to monthly rent and premium,
opens a new term of the original duration, and changes the state to \texttt{RENOVADA}.
Cancellation is logical and auditable.

The design optimizes for the following measurable quality attributes:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{3.1cm}L{3.5cm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{SoftGreen}
\textbf{Attribute} & \textbf{Initial target} & \textbf{Design implication} \\
\midrule
Availability & 99.95\% monthly API availability & Multi-zone stateless instances,
  health probes, no synchronous CORE dependency for accepted writes \\
Read latency & p95 below 300 ms & Indexed filters, pagination, read replicas when needed \\
Write latency & p95 below 600 ms & Local ACID transaction; external delivery is out-of-band \\
Durability & RPO near zero for accepted writes & Synchronous multi-zone database commit,
  point-in-time recovery, transactional outbox \\
Recovery & RTO below 30 minutes & Automated deployment, tested backups, runbooks,
  infrastructure as code \\
Security & No unauthenticated business operation & Gateway authentication plus
  service authorization; assessment API key is a minimal local control \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\textbf{Deployment choice.} Policy, Risk, and Renewal are logical services implemented as
modules in one Spring Boot deployable for the first release. Notification and the CORE
adapter may run as separate workers because they have different scaling and failure
profiles. If organizational ownership or traffic later requires extraction, module
interfaces become remote contracts without rewriting the domain.

\subsection{Three selected architectural patterns}

\begin{longtable}{@{}L{2.9cm}L{5.4cm}L{6.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{SoftGreen}
\textbf{Pattern} & \textbf{Why it fits} & \textbf{Trade-off and control} \\
\midrule
\endhead
\textbf{1. Modular monolith} &
Policy and Risk have related invariants and must change atomically. A single deployable
reduces network hops, distributed transactions, and 24/7 operational burden while still
separating ownership in code. &
Modules cannot scale independently at first. Track traffic and team coupling; extract a
module only when measurements justify the cost. Database access remains private to each
module through application interfaces. \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{2. Hexagonal architecture} &
Business rules depend on ports, not WebLogic, HTTP, JPA, or notification vendors.
The legacy CORE adapter is replaceable and can be tested with a fake. Controllers are
inbound adapters; repositories and CORE clients are outbound adapters. &
Adds interfaces and mapping code. Apply the pattern at real volatility boundaries
(CORE, notifications, persistence), not to every class. \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{3. Event-driven integration with transactional outbox} &
Creation and renewal notifications are naturally asynchronous. CORE availability must
not dictate platform availability. A policy mutation and its delivery intent commit
atomically, then workers deliver at least once. &
Consumers may see events later or more than once. Use event IDs, idempotent consumers,
bounded retry, a dead-letter state, lag alerts, and reconciliation with CORE. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Why not microservices immediately?} The domain boundaries are real, but the
assessment provides no traffic, team, or deployment evidence that compensates for
distributed consistency and on-call complexity. The proposed modules preserve the
option while choosing the safer first release.

\textbf{Key invariants}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \req{SD-04, BR-01}: an individual policy has exactly one risk; collective
  creation requires at least one and may accept more.
  \item \req{SD-06}: premium at creation is
  \(\textit{monthlyRent} \times \textit{initialDurationMonths}\).
  \item \req{SD-07, SD-08}: renewal opens a term with the original duration and applies
  \(1 + \textit{IPC}/100\) to rent and premium.
  \item \req{BR-02}: \texttt{CANCELADA} is terminal for renewal.
  \item \req{BR-03}: policy cancellation moves every risk to \texttt{CANCELADO} in the
  same transaction.
  \item Monetary values use fixed precision; rows use optimistic versions to detect
  concurrent changes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Critical write flow and CORE consistency}

For a renewal:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The gateway authenticates, rate-limits, and attaches a correlation ID.
  \item The Policy command loads the current row with its optimistic version.
  \item The domain rejects \texttt{CANCELADA}, validates IPC, recalculates values, and
  advances the effective period.
  \item One database transaction updates the policy and inserts a CORE outbox event.
  \item The API returns the committed policy. CORE latency is not on the request path.
  \item A worker reads due outbox events and calls the WebLogic agnostic edit service.
  \item Success marks \texttt{SENT}. Failure records the reason and schedules bounded
  exponential backoff. Exhausted events enter \texttt{FAILED} and alert operations.
\end{enumerate}

The delivery guarantee is \textbf{at least once}. The event UUID is the idempotency key.
WebLogic/CORE should store or recognize processed IDs. If it cannot, the adapter uses a
deduplication ledger. A scheduled reconciliation compares non-final outbox state and
CORE policy state, closing the unavoidable dual-system consistency gap.

\subsection{Automatic renewal and notifications}

A renewal scheduler queries eligible policies in bounded pages. Work is partitioned by
policy ID and claimed with a lease so multiple instances do not renew the same policy.
The normal domain command executes the renewal, which means manual and automatic paths
share all rules and CORE delivery behavior.

Creation and renewal also write a notification event to the same outbox abstraction.
The Notification Service independently delivers email and SMS. Provider failures never
roll back the policy; each channel owns retry policy, suppression, delivery receipts,
and a dead-letter queue. Message payloads reference policy IDs and template data rather
than exposing unnecessary personal information.

\subsection{Scalability, observability, fault tolerance, and API versioning}

\subsubsection{Scalability}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Run stateless API instances across at least two availability zones behind a
  load balancer; autoscale on CPU, request concurrency, and p95 latency.
  \item Paginate every list; use the composite policy \texttt{(type, status)} index and
  the risk \texttt{(policy\_id)} index. Add read replicas only after read pressure appears.
  \item Batch and partition renewal/outbox workers; apply backpressure when CORE or
  notification providers slow down.
  \item Cache stable reference/configuration data, not mutable policy state by default.
  \item Extract Notification or Risk only when independent scaling, ownership, or
  deployment cadence provides measured value.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Logs and observability}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Emit structured JSON in production with timestamp, severity, service, version,
  environment, trace ID, policy ID, event ID, route, duration, and outcome.
  \item Propagate W3C trace context through Gateway, API, broker, notification, WebLogic,
  and CORE adapter. Never log API keys, phone numbers, or full sensitive payloads.
  \item RED metrics: request rate, error rate, and duration per route. Domain metrics:
  renewals, cancellations, risk additions, CORE success/failure, outbox lag and age.
  \item SLO alerts should use burn rate, not isolated thresholds. Page for availability,
  high error budget burn, stuck outbox, or reconciliation drift; ticket slow trends.
  \item Dashboards link metrics to traces and structured logs. Every alert has an owner
  and a short runbook.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Fault tolerance}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Strict connect/read timeouts on WebLogic, email, and SMS; retry only transient,
  idempotent operations with jitter. Circuit breakers prevent dependency failure storms.
  \item Transactional outbox prevents lost events. Dead-letter state, replay tooling,
  and reconciliation address long outages and poison events.
  \item Optimistic locking rejects conflicting writes with HTTP 409 instead of silently
  overwriting state.
  \item PostgreSQL uses multi-zone replication, encrypted backups, point-in-time recovery,
  and regularly tested restore procedures.
  \item Graceful shutdown, liveness/readiness probes, rolling deployment, and minimum
  healthy capacity preserve 24/7 service.
  \item Chaos exercises cover CORE timeout, broker lag, database failover, and provider
  outage before production launch.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{API versioning}

The public production contract begins at \texttt{/api/v1}; the exact unversioned routes
remain in the assessment implementation because they are explicitly required. Breaking
changes use a new major path. Compatible field additions stay in the current version.
OpenAPI is the contract, consumer-driven tests detect incompatibility, and gateway
analytics identify active clients. A version receives a published deprecation window,
\texttt{Deprecation} and \texttt{Sunset} headers, a migration guide, and an observed
retirement date.

\subsection{Security and privacy beyond the minimum}

The required \texttt{x-api-key} is implemented using constant-time comparison and is
externalized through \texttt{POLICY\_API\_KEY}; the literal \texttt{123456} exists only
as the mandated local default. Production uses OAuth2/OIDC at the gateway, short-lived
service credentials, role/ownership authorization, secret rotation, TLS/mTLS, encryption
at rest, input and payload limits, rate limiting, audit logs, dependency scanning, and
least-privilege database roles. Colombian personal data is minimized, retained by policy,
masked in non-production, and excluded from logs and messages unless required.

\subsection{Implementation decisions that protect correctness}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Aggregate methods own rules.} Invalid transitions cannot be bypassed by a
  controller or future scheduler.
  \item \textbf{Financial precision is explicit.} \texttt{BigDecimal}, two decimal places,
  and \texttt{HALF\_UP} avoid floating-point errors.
  \item \textbf{API errors are stable.} Problems contain HTTP status, machine code, path,
  timestamp, and field errors without leaking internals.
  \item \textbf{Concurrency is visible.} JPA versions produce 409 on conflicting updates.
  \item \textbf{CORE intent is atomic.} A mutation cannot commit without its outbox row;
  delivery failure does not lose the change.
  \item \textbf{The evaluator can inspect behavior.} OpenAPI, sample data, health, metrics,
  Docker, and tests minimize setup uncertainty.
\end{itemize}

The assessment keeps focus on the essential code. Before production, add gateway OIDC
and authorization, notification outbox/schema, distributed outbox claiming, a real
broker, immutable business audit history, PostgreSQL integration tests with Testcontainers,
contract tests against WebLogic, secret manager integration, and performance/chaos tests.
These are explicit extensions, not hidden assumptions.

\section{Module 3 - Database Optimization}

\subsection{Problem framing}

The frequent query joins 10 million orders to 500,000 customers and filters customers by
country:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
SELECT
  o.order_id,
  o.order_date,
  c.customer_name,
  o.total_amount
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
WHERE c.country = 'México';
\end{lstlisting}

The likely cost is a scan of customers to find Mexico followed by repeated or broad
access to orders. The right choice depends on selectivity, distribution, result size,
storage engine, and the actual plan. I would first collect a baseline with
\texttt{EXPLAIN ANALYZE} (or Oracle \texttt{DBMS\_XPLAN.DISPLAY\_CURSOR}) and measure
logical reads, physical reads, row estimates, join algorithm, spills, and p95 duration.

\subsection{Strategy 1 - Composite and covering indexes}

At minimum:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
CREATE INDEX idx_customers_country_customer
  ON customers(country, customer_id);

CREATE INDEX idx_orders_customer
  ON orders(customer_id);
\end{lstlisting}

The first index rapidly finds Mexico customer IDs; the second supports indexed joins to
their orders. On engines with included columns, reduce table lookups:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
-- PostgreSQL example
CREATE INDEX idx_customers_country_cover
  ON customers(country, customer_id)
  INCLUDE (customer_name);

CREATE INDEX idx_orders_customer_cover
  ON orders(customer_id)
  INCLUDE (order_id, order_date, total_amount);
\end{lstlisting}

I would not blindly create every covering index: on 10 million rows it increases storage,
cache pressure, and write cost. Validate benefit with the real plan and workload, remove
redundant indexes, and rebuild/reorganize only when measured fragmentation warrants it.

\subsection{Strategy 2 - Accurate statistics and plan validation}

Indexes fail when the optimizer estimates country selectivity incorrectly. Refresh
statistics and histograms for \texttt{customers.country}, and statistics for join keys.
In Oracle, use \texttt{DBMS\_STATS.GATHER\_TABLE\_STATS} with an appropriate histogram;
in PostgreSQL, run \texttt{ANALYZE} and raise the statistics target for skewed country data.

Then confirm the plan begins with filtered customers and uses an indexed nested-loop join
when Mexico is selective, or a hash join when it is not. Check for implicit conversions,
collation/Unicode mismatches, functions on \texttt{country}, stale bind peeking, and
insufficient work memory causing hash/sort spills. Do not force a join hint until the
root estimation problem is understood.

\subsection{Strategy 3 - Materialized read model for a frequent expensive report}

If this query is read frequently but the acceptable freshness is minutes rather than
milliseconds, maintain a materialized view or reporting table containing the joined
projection:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW orders_by_country AS
SELECT
  c.country,
  o.order_id,
  o.order_date,
  c.customer_name,
  o.total_amount
FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;

CREATE INDEX idx_orders_by_country
  ON orders_by_country(country, order_date);
\end{lstlisting}

Refresh incrementally/fast where the engine permits, or populate through change-data
capture. This trades write/refresh complexity and controlled staleness for predictable
read speed and isolates analytical load from the transactional path.

\subsection{Strategy 4 - Denormalize/partition only at proven scale}

Country belongs to Customer, so ordinary partitioning of Orders by country is impossible
without denormalization. If country reports dominate and indexes/read models remain
insufficient, copy a stable \texttt{customer\_country} onto Orders through a controlled
event/CDC process, then partition by country or region. Partition pruning can eliminate
most of 10 million rows.

This is a later option: customer-country changes require backfill and correctness controls,
and too many small country partitions can harm operations. Date partitioning may help
retention and recent-period queries but does \emph{not} optimize the supplied unbounded
query by itself.

\subsection{Strategy 5 - Bound result cost and separate workloads}

The result itself may contain millions of rows; no index makes transmitting every row
cheap. Clarify the use case, add a date range and keyset pagination when valid, return
aggregates for dashboards, or export asynchronously. Route reporting to a read replica
or warehouse, configure adequate buffer cache and I/O, and enable parallel query only
after concurrency tests. The success metric is end-to-end latency and database load,
not merely a lower estimated plan cost.

\subsection{Recommended execution order}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Capture the current plan and workload baseline.
  \item Add/validate the two join/filter indexes and refresh statistics.
  \item Check cardinality, result size, and plan stability under representative parameters.
  \item If the workload is still expensive, create a materialized read model and move
  reporting away from the OLTP primary.
  \item Consider denormalization/partitioning only with measured evidence and ownership
  of the consistency cost.
\end{enumerate}

This gives five distinct strategies and prioritizes reversible, low-risk improvements
before structural changes.

\section{Module 4 - Git and GitHub}

\subsection{Command and justification}

Use \textbf{\texttt{git cherry-pick}} because the requirement is to bring one specific
security-fix commit from \texttt{main}, not the other updates:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
git fetch origin
git switch feature/new-login
git status --short
git cherry-pick -x <security-fix-commit-sha>
\end{lstlisting}

\texttt{-x} appends the original commit SHA to the new commit message, preserving an
audit trail that is especially useful for a security fix. Cherry-pick applies only the
selected patch and creates a new commit on \texttt{feature/new-login}; merge or rebase
would incorporate unrelated \texttt{main} history and violate the requirement.

\subsection{Safe operational sequence}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Confirm the worktree is clean and inspect the source commit:
  \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
git show --stat --oneline <security-fix-commit-sha>
  \end{lstlisting}
  \item Cherry-pick with \texttt{-x}. If conflicts occur, resolve only after understanding
  both security intent and feature behavior, stage resolved files, then:
  \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
git cherry-pick --continue
  \end{lstlisting}
  Abort safely with \texttt{git cherry-pick --abort} if the resolution is uncertain.
  \item Run security-specific tests and the complete quality gate:
  \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
mvn verify
  \end{lstlisting}
  \item Push the branch and explain the backport in the pull request. Do not modify the
  original \texttt{main} commit.
\end{enumerate}

If the security fix is composed of multiple inseparable commits, cherry-pick the ordered
range or each required SHA, after confirming with the fix owner. The strategy remains
selective rather than merging all of \texttt{main}.

\subsection{Situation assessment}

Ten critical incidents in one month show that reliability is already harming customers
and delivery capacity. Forty percent technical debt, missing standards, and irregular
review make a fixed three-week feature promise unsafe. The response is not to stop all
delivery or accept uncontrolled risk: stabilize the highest-risk paths, reduce feature
scope to a testable vertical slice, install a lightweight quality system, and make the
trade-offs visible to the business.

\subsection{Team organization for speed and quality}

Organize eight developers into two outcome-oriented pods while keeping one shared
technical system:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Feature pod (4):} senior technical owner, two developers, and one junior.
  Own the thin vertical slice end-to-end, including tests, telemetry, and rollout.
  \item \textbf{Reliability/platform pod (3):} senior owner plus two developers. Own top
  incident causes, CI, service safeguards, runbooks, and debt on the delivery path.
  \item \textbf{Rotating incident captain (1 at a time):} shields both pods from
  interruptions, coordinates incidents, and returns to normal work after the rotation.
  This is a role drawn from the eight, not a ninth person.
\end{itemize}

Use a single prioritized board and daily 15-minute risk sync. Work in small batches;
limit work in progress; swarm on blocked/high-risk tasks. Each important area has a
primary and secondary owner. Juniors pair on real tasks with shrinking support rather
than receiving isolated training projects. Seniors rotate review and pairing so
mentoring does not become one person's permanent tax.

\subsection{Mandatory technical practices}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Protected main branch; no direct pushes; CI must compile, test, scan dependencies,
  and enforce formatting/static analysis.
  \item At least one meaningful reviewer; two or a code-owner review for critical/security
  changes. Small PRs with context, risk, test evidence, and rollback notes.
  \item Definition of Done: acceptance tests, automated tests at the right level,
  telemetry, security/privacy review, documentation, feature flag/rollback, and no new
  unowned high-severity issue.
  \item Trunk-oriented short-lived branches, frequent integration, and feature flags.
  \item Production readiness for critical services: SLO, dashboard, actionable alert,
  runbook, owner, capacity check, backup/restore proof, and dependency timeout behavior.
  \item Blameless incident response with severity, incident commander, timeline,
  communication intervals, learning review, and tracked actions.
  \item Architecture Decision Records for material choices; threat modeling for new data
  flows; secret scanning and least privilege.
\end{enumerate}

Emergency changes may use an expedited review, but never skip traceability and automated
smoke tests; retrospective review follows within one business day.

\subsection{Managing business pressure without compromising quality}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Translate risk into options.} Present a three-option decision:
  (A) thin safe slice in three weeks, (B) broader scope with a later date, or
  (C) controlled beta in three weeks with restricted users. Quantify customer, incident,
  and opportunity impact.
  \item \textbf{Fix quality floors, flex scope.} Security, data correctness, observability,
  rollback, and tests are non-negotiable. Nice-to-have journeys and polish move behind
  feature flags or to the next increment.
  \item \textbf{Deliver evidence every few days.} Demo a walking skeleton early, test
  risky integrations first, and publish forecast/risk updates twice weekly.
  \item \textbf{Use progressive delivery.} Dark launch, internal users, 5\% canary,
  monitored ramp, explicit stop conditions, and rapid rollback.
  \item \textbf{Escalate decisions, not anxiety.} When scope/date/quality cannot all hold,
  document the trade-off, recommend one option, name the accountable decision-maker,
  and record the decision.
\end{enumerate}

For this case I recommend the thin vertical slice in three weeks, reserving roughly
30\% capacity for reliability/debt during weeks one and two, then adjusting from incident
load and feature risk. A critical SLO burn or unresolved security issue triggers a
release stop. This protects customers and gives the business a predictable, demonstrable
outcome rather than an optimistic scope promise.
